# The hackathon: build it, then show it

Saturday 21 November 2026 · 09:00 to 15:00 IST

## How the day works

- Pick one of the four enterprise scenarios, or bring your own. Tell the chat your name and your number by 09:30.
- Build your own answer, **alone**, from an empty folder, as a **small web app**. Use any language, any tool and any AI you like. You build from 09:20 to 13:00, with breaks at 10:40 and at 12:00.
- If you want to show your work, type **DEMO** in the chat by 12:45. Nobody is made to, and it does not have to be finished.
- At 13:05 the people who signed up show what they built, one by one: **5 minutes to demo, 2 minutes for questions.** Everyone else is the audience.
- Every demo is scored live against the rubric on the next page: **100 points**. There is a leaderboard at the end.
- Nobody will tell you how to build it. The design decisions are yours, and so are the mistakes.

## The rules

1. **Work alone.** Ask the chat only for what is broken (your laptop, your access), not for design advice.
2. **Start from nothing.** There is no starter code. The sample data is in the scenario folder.
3. **Show your web app working, live. No slides.** One simple page is enough, and it can run on your own laptop: you do not need to deploy it. A non-developer should be able to use it. Keep one saved result open in a tab as a plan B in case the live run fails.
4. **Be honest about the edges.** Part of your demo is where it fails.
5. **Keep a rough note of what your AI use cost.** You will say it out loud if you show your work.
6. **Use only the data in the folder.** Never put client data, personal data or a secret key on screen. That is a red line.

## What you say in your five minutes

1. What your web app does, who it is for, and a live run on the sample data.
2. One design decision you made, and the option you did not take.
3. Where it fails, what it says when it does not know, and how you would know it is wrong.
4. How AI helped, one thing it got wrong that you caught, and roughly what it cost.
5. Who would use it first, what a client would require before using it, and what you would do next.

## What you need

Your laptop, a screen-share that works, and your AI tools. That is all. The scoring page comes next, then the scenarios, one to a page.
